# Report outline

Each section lists the evidence it relies on. Figures are in `figures/`, tables in
`results/summary/REPORT_TABLES.md`; both are regenerated by `make figures tables`.

## 1. Introduction
- Urban congestion, why signals matter, the brief's "How might we" question.
- Contributions: cooperative MARL with a safety shield; evaluation beyond flow
  (emergency, CO2, pedestrians, buses, incidents, failures); honest comparison with
  Max-Pressure; zero-shot transfer to a larger network.

## 2. Design Thinking process
- Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test (`docs/design_thinking/`), including the
  interviews and field observation done by the team.

## 3. Related work
- Classical control: Webster (1958) fixed-time; actuated control; SOTL (Gershenson 2005).
- Max-Pressure / back-pressure: Varaiya (2013).
- RL for signal control: IntelliLight (Wei et al. 2018), PressLight (Wei et al. 2019),
  FRAP (Zheng et al. 2019), MPLight (Chen et al. 2020), MA2C (Chu et al. 2019),
  CoLight (Wei et al. 2019).
- Benchmarks: RESCO (Ault and Sharon 2021), sumo-rl.
- Positioning: parameter sharing + explicit neighbour messages + shield + multi-criteria
  evaluation.

## 4. Problem formalisation
- Dec-POMDP: agents, observation blocks, action = next green phase, transition = SUMO.
- Reward: r = -Δ(person-weighted waiting)/n_wait - λ_co2·CO2/n_co2 - λ_ev·EVwait/n_ev - λ_ped·PEDwait/n_ped (ADR on reward design).
- PPO objective with parameter sharing; why sharing enables transfer.

## 5. System architecture
- Diagram from `docs/design_thinking/prototype.md`; environment, shield, controllers,
  evaluation pipeline; reproducibility (configs, seeds, git hash in every result row).

## 6. Agents and baselines
- Observation design (ADR), reward design and weights (ADR), cooperation mechanism (ADR),
  safety shield (ADR-003), heterogeneity padding (ADR).
- Baselines: fixed-time Webster, actuated, Max-Pressure, EV preemption rule (ADR).

## 7. Experimental setup
- Networks (grid4x4, real district, grid8x8), demand calibration, scenarios, episode
  protocol, seeds, metrics definitions (`src/cotraffic/evaluation/metrics.py`),
  statistics, compute budget and measured wall times.

## 8. Results (one subsection per research question)
- RQ1 Do cooperative agents outperform independent agents and classical controllers?
  Figures 1, 2, 3, 10; main table.
- RQ2 Can emergency vehicles be prioritised without significantly degrading overall
  traffic? Figure 5; EV table.
- RQ3 What is the trade-off between travel time and CO2? Figure 4 (Pareto front).
- RQ4 How robust is the system to accidents, sensor noise and communication failures?
  Figures 6 and 8.
- RQ5 Does the approach scale to larger networks without retraining? Figure 9.
- RQ6 (Tier 3) Does short-term forecasting improve decisions? Forecast table.
- People: pedestrians and buses (Figure 11). Adaptivity: rush profile (Figure 7).
- Explainability: feature-group importance and decision logs (Figure 12).

## 9. Ablations
- IND vs COOP; COOP vs COOP-ECO (λ = 0, 0.5, 1.0); COOP vs COOP-FULL vs EV-RULE on
  emergencies; (Tier 3) with vs without forecast.

## 10. Limitations
- From `docs/FAILURES.md` and the negative results: where the agents lose, why.
- Simulation-only; synthetic demand unless real counts; simplified pedestrian clearance
  and emergency-vehicle behaviour; single district.

## 11. Deployment considerations
- Sim-to-real gap (calibration, detector placement), fail-safe fallback to actuated
  control, edge hardware and measured inference latency, privacy (aggregate counts only,
  no tracking of individuals), phased roll-out with shadow mode.
