## Stakeholder map

| Stakeholder | What they experience | What we want to learn from them |
|---|---|---|
| Car drivers / commuters | Queues, stop-and-go, unpredictable trip times | Where and when delays hurt most; what "a good signal" means to them |
| Bus users and bus operator | Buses stuck in the same queues as cars; bunching | How much delay per trip; whether priority would matter |
| Pedestrians | Long waits at crossings, unsafe crossing behaviour | Acceptable waiting time; crossings they avoid and why |
| Emergency services (ambulance, civil protection, fire) | Blocked by queues at red lights | Typical delay at signals; how drivers react; current practice |
| Municipal traffic department | Operates fixed plans, few sensors | Which data exists (counts, loops, cameras); constraints for deployment |
| Residents / environment | Noise and pollution near congested junctions | Perception of pollution hot spots |
| Police / traffic officers | Manual control at peak hours | When and why they override signals |

## Interview guide (15-20 min per person)

1. Describe your usual trip through the district. Where do you lose time?
2. When did a traffic light last feel "wrong" to you? What happened?
3. How do you react to a long red when the crossing road is empty?
4. (Bus / emergency) How often are you delayed at signals? For how long?
5. (Pedestrians) How long are you willing to wait before crossing on red?
6. (City staff) What data do you collect? Who changes signal plans and how often?
7. Would you trust a signal that adapts automatically? What would make you trust it?
